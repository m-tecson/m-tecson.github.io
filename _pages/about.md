I'm a structural biochemist transitioning to early-stage drug discovery. I graduated from San Francisco State University (SFSU) with my Bachelor's in Biochemistry and my Master's in Chemistry (concentration in Biochemistry). At SFSU, I studied how human glutamine synthetase (GS) is regulated by combining cryogenic electron microscopy (cryo-EM), enzyme kinetics, and molecular dynamics (MD) ensemble fitting approaches to understand how GS structure and function are connected.

My research was completed under the training and mentorship of [Dr. Eric Greene](https://www.egreenelab.org/) and culminated in several key works. As an undergraduate student I had published in [*Biochemistry*](/publication/2025-01-23-GS-biochem-perspective) as a co-first author a perspective regarding the diverse functions and regulatory mechanisms of glutamine synthetases. Additionally, I had contributed to understanding the allosteric regulation of GS through filamentation which was published by [*eLife*](/publication/2026-10-12-GS-filament-paper) in October 2026. I also have three manuscripts in preparation exploring disease-associated mutations of human GS, the functional impact of assembly status in human GS, as well as understanding post-translational modifications in *E. coli* GS.

As a founding member of the Greene Lab, I helped build the lab’s day-to-day infrastructure as it grew. I mentored 9 new researchers to the point of running experiments independently, managed the chemical inventory, maintained the lab’s centralized protocols, and troubleshot equipment ranging from ÄKTA protein purification systems to lab workstations and computing resources.

Starting November 16, I’m joining [CxT Discovery](https://cxtdiscovery.org/) as a Staff Research Associate, where I’ll run biochemical and biophysical assays and support structure-activity relationship (SAR) investigations across the early-stage discovery pipeline. I’ll bring my background in protein biochemistry and enzymology to that work, along with a structural biology perspective. I’m eager to learn structure-guided design and to grow into a scientist who can help move discovery projects forward, with a PhD as my longer-term goal.

My research training was supported by several pre-doctoral fellowship programs, including:

- NIH Undergraduate Research Training Initiative for Student Enhancement (U-RISE)
- NIH Bridges to the Doctorate (B2D, Bridges)
- Genentech Foundation Master of Science Scholars
- NSF Louis Stokes Alliance for Minority Participation in STEM (LSAMP)

These programs provided tuition coverage and a stipend to support my research at SFSU.
