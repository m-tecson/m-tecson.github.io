**Abstract:** Enzymes are proteins that catalyze essential biochemical reactions by transitioning between different conformations to facilitate each step. These conformational changes can be influenced by factors distant from the active site, a phenomenon known as allosteric regulation. Allosteric regulation allows enzymes to be finely tuned by various molecules or structural states, enhancing or inhibiting their activity to meet cellular needs. This project investigates the allosteric pathways regulating human glutamine synthetase (HsGS), a crucial enzyme in nitrogen metabolism with implications in various diseases. We map an allosteric network by analyzing conformational states generated through cyrogenic electron microscopy (cryo-EM), molecular dynamics (MD) ensemble fitting, and graph theory methods. Preliminary kinetic data demonstrates that mutations at a crucial oligomeric state interface negatively impact Michaelis-Menten paramters, illustrating the coupling of structural composition to enzyme activity. Our findings reveal a comprehensive allosteric network in wild-type HsGS systems that facilitates communication across this conserved interface, which is absent in an enzyme variant mutated near this interface. Defining the importance of this oligomeric interface of GS presents a promising target for developing novel therapeutics with dual applications. In cancer treatment, disrupting this interface could lead to the development of selective GS inhibitors, potentially starving tumors of the glutamine crucial for their growth and survival. Conversely, in neurodegenerative disorders, where GS activity is often diminished, promoting or stabilizing this oligomeric interface could enhance GS function, potentially mitigating the harmful effects of reduced glutamine synthesis and accumulation of neurotoxic ammonia.
